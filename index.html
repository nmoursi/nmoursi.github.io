About

I study how people interact with AI systems, and how we can design those systems to better support human goals, judgment, and behavior.

My research sits at the intersection of human-computer interaction and human-centered AI, with current work on AI agents, adaptive systems, and tools that support people in complex research and learning tasks.

I'm a master's student and Research Assistant in the Human-Centered AI Lab at Northeastern University, working with Prof. Dakuo Wang. I approach these questions through a combination of system building, qualitative research, and large-scale behavioral experiments — from building agentic tools that help UX researchers analyze and revise usability studies, to evaluating AI-driven personalization through randomized experiments in a live online environment.

Before graduate school I was a Product Manager at Microsoft, designing and evaluating consumer product experiences through experimentation and behavioral analysis. That's where my interest in studying what people actually do — rather than what they report — began.

Research Interests

I'm broadly interested in designing and studying human-centered AI systems that adapt to people while preserving human agency and judgment. Three questions drive my work:

Human–AI Interaction

How do people understand, trust, and work with increasingly agentic AI systems, and how should these systems communicate their capabilities and limitations?

Adaptive & Personalized AI

When does adaptation meaningfully improve people's experiences and outcomes, and how should systems respond to differences in users' goals, motivations, and behavior?

AI as a Research Partner

How can AI agents support complex knowledge work such as UX research without obscuring evidence, uncertainty, or human judgment?

Research

UXAgent V2

Designing AI Agents to Support UX Research

Human–AI Interaction · LLM Agents · UX Research · Qualitative Methods

Research question How can AI agents help UX researchers identify problems in a usability study before recruiting human participants, while keeping researchers in control of interpretation and evidence?

UXAgent is an LLM-agent system for simulating and analyzing usability studies. In V2 we investigate a broader question: rather than replacing human participants, can simulated interactions serve as an early design-feedback mechanism that helps researchers find weaknesses and revise study protocols before running human pilots?

My contributions

I work across both the research and system sides of V2. I contributed to the literature review and study framing, designed the qualitative-analysis architecture, and implemented a three-stage agentic coding pipeline that transforms interaction traces into session-level codes, coordinates codes across sessions into a consolidated codebook, and produces evidence-grounded cross-session summaries.

  1. Interaction traces
  2. Session coding
  3. Cross-session coordination
  4. Codebook
  5. Evidence-grounded findings

I also built the researcher-facing mechanisms for inspecting and revising generated codes, and the evidence-grounding requirements that tie every reported finding back to code prevalence and the supporting interaction evidence — so a researcher can audit a claim rather than trust it.

Alongside the system, I run the empirical work that tests whether any of it holds up: the evaluation study protocol and its gold-standard quality target, and a formative interview study with UX practitioners on how study designs actually get validated today, and where pilots fall short.

Adaptive Learning in the Wild

When Does AI Personalization Actually Help?

First author · Submitted to KDD 2027

Research question Does adapting an experience to a user's estimated knowledge actually improve engagement and learning in a real-world environment?

We study this through a randomized field experiment in a live Roblox financial-literacy game, comparing a fixed progression against AI-driven quest selection using Bayesian Knowledge Tracing — in collaboration with Carnegie Mellon University and U.S. Bank.

My role

I lead the study framing, statistical analysis, interpretation, and manuscript development across engagement, assessment performance, skill progression, motivation, and reward-participation outcomes.

Methods Randomized field experiment · Regression · Interaction analysis · Heterogeneous effects · Behavioral telemetry
