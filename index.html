About

Hi! I'm Nada. I'm a master's student in Applied Artificial Intelligence at Northeastern University and a Research Assistant in the Human-Centered AI Lab, under the supervision of Prof. Dakuo Wang.

My research is in human-computer interaction and human-centered AI, with a focus on how AI systems can adapt to people while supporting human judgment and agency. I've been exploring this through AI agents that support UX researchers in evaluating and refining usability studies, and through large-scale randomized experiments studying AI-driven personalization in real-world environments.

More broadly, I'm interested in human-AI interaction, adaptive and personalized systems, AI-supported research and design, and applications of human-centered AI to learning and well-being.

Before graduate school, I worked in product at Microsoft, which shaped my interest in understanding how intelligent systems behave once they become part of real products and people's everyday experiences.

Research

UXAgent V2

AI Agents for Supporting UX Research

How can AI agents help researchers identify weaknesses in usability studies while preserving human judgment and traceability?

UXAgent lets UX researchers dry-run a usability study with simulated participants before recruiting real ones. In V2 I work on the part that decides whether the output is trustworthy: how simulated sessions get analyzed, and whether the findings that come out can be traced back to evidence a researcher can actually check.

My contributions

I contributed to the literature review and study framing, designed the qualitative-analysis architecture, and implemented a three-stage coding pipeline that turns interaction traces into session-level codes, coordinates those codes across sessions into a consolidated codebook, and produces evidence-grounded summaries.

  1. Interaction traces
  2. Session coding
  3. Cross-session coordination
  4. Codebook
  5. Evidence-grounded findings

I also built the researcher-facing tools for reviewing and revising the generated codes, and I run the empirical work around the system: an evaluation study of the analysis quality, and a formative interview study with UX practitioners on how study designs get validated today.

Adaptive Learning in the Wild

Evaluating AI Personalization Through a Randomized Field Experiment

When does adapting an experience to a person's estimated knowledge actually improve engagement and learning?

A randomized field experiment in a live Roblox financial-literacy game, comparing a fixed quest progression against AI-driven quest selection using Bayesian Knowledge Tracing, in collaboration with Carnegie Mellon University and U.S. Bank.

My role

I lead the study framing, statistical analysis, interpretation, and manuscript development across engagement, assessment performance, skill progression, motivation, and reward-participation outcomes.

Methods Randomized field experiment · Regression · Interaction analysis · Heterogeneous effects · Behavioral telemetry

Automating Research Intelligence: Advanced-AI-Generated vs Manually Designed Pipelines

Ruthvik Nath Bandari, Shivam Indraprasad Dubey, Yaqi Guo, Kunal Gupta, Anish Indurthy, Yash Mahavir Jain, Yun-Tang Lin, Ching-Yi Mao, Nada Moursi, Om Manojkumar Patel, Kapil Varma, Jialin Wang, Kasin Wasuwanich, Siqi Xiang, Yucheng Yan, Sijia Zhan, Anton V. Sinitskiy

Equal-contribution first co-authors, listed alphabetically

TechRxiv preprint · 2026 Preprint ↗

An empirical comparison of agentic AI frameworks — AutoGen, AutoGPT, OpenAgents, OpenHands, Claude, and Gemini — against manually engineered pipelines on the same research-intelligence tasks. The frameworks produce professional-looking code quickly, but none yielded a working pipeline without extensive human intervention, which motivates hybrid designs that use AI for bounded, verifiable subtasks inside deterministic backbones.
