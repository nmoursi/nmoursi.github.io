About

Hi! I'm Nada. I'm a master's student in Applied Artificial Intelligence at Northeastern University and a Research Assistant in the Human-Centered AI Lab, advised by Prof. Dakuo Wang.

My research is in human-computer interaction and human-centered AI, focusing on how adaptive and agentic AI systems can support people while preserving human judgment and agency. I study these questions through AI agents for UX research and large-scale experiments of AI-driven personalization in deployed environments.

Before graduate school, I worked in product at Microsoft, which shaped my interest in studying how intelligent systems interact with people in real-world products.

Research

UXAgent

AI Agents for Supporting UX Research

How can AI agents support UX researchers while keeping analysis inspectable and researchers in control?

UXAgent is an LLM-agent system for simulating usability studies before human pilots. I contribute across V1 and V2, from qualitative analysis to the design and implementation of V2's agentic analysis pipeline.

My work. I developed session-level coding, cross-session coordination, Codebook and Report agent workflows, evidence-grounded reporting, and human-in-the-loop codebook review. I also conducted formative research with UX practitioners and an empirical evaluation of the system.

Methods Formative interviews · Qualitative analysis · LLM agents · System design · Empirical evaluation

Adaptive Learning in the Wild

Evaluating Adaptive AI in the Wild

When does AI-driven personalization improve people's experiences and outcomes, and for whom?

As first author, I study this through a randomized field experiment in a live Roblox learning environment — 31,603 players randomized, with engagement analyses on 1,778 survey completers — comparing fixed progression with adaptive quest selection using Bayesian Knowledge Tracing.

I lead the statistical analysis and manuscript development, including heterogeneous-effects analyses examining how personalization varies across motivation, behavioral engagement, and reward participation.

Methods Randomized field experiment · Bayesian Knowledge Tracing · Regression · Heterogeneous effects · Behavioral telemetry
