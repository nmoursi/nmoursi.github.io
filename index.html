About

I study what AI systems actually do for people once they leave the lab — and I measure it with randomized experiments in live products.

I'm a master's student in Applied Artificial Intelligence at Northeastern University and a Research Assistant in the Human-Centered AI Lab, where I work with Prof. Dakuo Wang on human–AI interaction, AI-agent evaluation, adaptive learning, and the responsible deployment of AI-driven systems.

My main project leads the quantitative analysis for a CMU and U.S. Bank collaboration evaluating AI-driven quest personalization inside a live Roblox financial-literacy game — a randomized field trial rather than a benchmark. Alongside it, I contribute qualitative coding and thematic analysis to UXAgent, which uses LLM agents as simulated usability-testing participants.

Before graduate school I spent several years shipping products — most recently as a Product Manager at Microsoft across Skype and Microsoft Clarity, running A/B experiments and onboarding analyses. That's where the interest in measuring real outcomes, rather than reported ones, began.

Research & Publications

Adaptive Learning in the Wild: A Randomized Trial of AI-Driven Quest Selection on Roblox

Nada Moursi (first author)

Submitted · KDD 2027

A live randomized trial comparing fixed quest sequencing against Bayesian Knowledge Tracing–based personalization, measured across engagement, assessment performance, skill change, motivation, and reward participation. I lead the analysis — regression, interaction, subgroup, and robustness models on randomized experimental data — in collaboration with Carnegie Mellon University and U.S. Bank.

UXAgent: Simulating Usability Testing of Web Design with LLM Agents as Participants

Co-author

In preparation

An LLM-agent system that lets UX researchers dry-run usability studies with simulated participants interacting with live websites. I contribute qualitative coding and thematic analysis of participant behavior, trust, and perceptions of AI-agent systems.
