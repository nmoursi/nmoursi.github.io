About

I study what AI systems actually do for people once they leave the lab — and I measure it with randomized experiments in live products.

I'm a master's student in Applied Artificial Intelligence at Northeastern University and a Research Assistant in the Human-Centered AI Lab, where I work with Prof. Dakuo Wang on human–AI interaction, AI-agent evaluation, adaptive learning, and the responsible deployment of AI-driven systems.

My work runs in two directions. One is measurement: I lead the quantitative analysis for a CMU and U.S. Bank collaboration evaluating AI-driven quest personalization inside a live Roblox financial-literacy game — a randomized field trial, not a benchmark. The other is method: for UXAgent v2 I built the qualitative analysis pipeline that turns LLM-agent interaction traces into evidence a UX researcher can actually defend, and I run the formative and evaluation studies that test whether any of it holds up.

Before graduate school I spent several years shipping products — most recently as a Product Manager at Microsoft across Skype and Microsoft Clarity, running A/B experiments and onboarding analyses. That's where the interest in measuring real outcomes, rather than reported ones, began.

UXAgent v2 — What I Built

Simulated participants are easy. Trustworthy findings from simulated participants are the hard part. For v2 I built the analysis and evaluation layer that closes that gap — the pipeline that turns agent traces into auditable evidence, and the studies that test whether it holds up.

Pipeline

Qualitative coding, three stages

Designed and implemented the pipeline that turns raw agent interaction traces into analyzable qualitative data: per-session coding → cross-session coordination → coded summary. Each stage has a written spec, a typed schema, and a CLI, so a run is reproducible rather than a one-off prompt.

session_codes.json consolidated_codebook.json trace_summary.json

Evidence

A codebook you can audit

The consolidated codebook carries prevalence counts (k/N), affected runs, and representative quotes for every code — so a finding in the final report traces back to specific turns in specific sessions. I added quality rules that stop large studies from collapsing into universal themes, filtering tautological and non-behavioral codes.

Human oversight

The researcher stays in the loop

Built the human review layer — approve, flag, or rewrite any code definition before it reaches the report — plus a codebook assistant and a written human-oversight spec for what happens when a researcher disagrees with an auto-generated finding. Structured feedback and revision, not hand-editing Markdown.

Evaluation

UXAgent evaluation study protocol

Authored the evaluation protocol and a gold-standard report for a pilot study — CHI-style findings with evidence, prevalence, limitations, and recommendations — used as the quality target the analysis agent is scored against, together with a report-quality rubric and a minimum-N policy for demographic segment claims.

Formative study

Understanding challenges in designing and validating usability studies

An interview study with UX practitioners on how study designs actually get validated today — where pilots fall short, what goes uncaught until the main study, and which of those gaps a pre-study simulation could plausibly close. This is the empirical grounding for the system's motivation.

Literature

Why pilots can't validate a study design

Built the argument from the methodology literature: pilot studies are formally defined as feasibility instruments, and their results are statistically uninterpretable as design validation. That reframing is what motivates a pre-study feedback loop — and it's the gap UXAgent is positioned to fill.
